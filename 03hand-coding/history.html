18th Cenutry

Food Guilds

Before restaurants, in France, everything was broken up into Food Guilds. Each Guild would have a monopoly on each food catergory. For example, Pâtissiers would do poultry, pies, and tarts, and Traiteurs would do meat stews. This system ensured profitability and high quality standards. At the time, it was unlawful for a tavern owner to produce their own food instead of buying from the guilds, and that's what made Boulanger so important.

Monsieur Boulanger's Restaurant

Boulanger was a small tavern owner, and one day he advertised Sheep's Feet in White Sauce, and called it a restauer or restorative in French. The Food Guilds saw this as an attack on their monopoly and sued Boulanger, but he was able to win by arguing that it was not a soup because the sheep's feet were served in sauce, not cooked in it. Later the term restauer evolved into restaurant, being used to describe the establisment, rather than the dish.

19th Century

Grande Cuisine & Carême

Grande Cuisine notably reached its peak in the hands of Antoine Carême. Grande Cuisine consited of elaborate multi-course meals that had extremely intricate preparation and garnishing. Carême also standardized the use of a traditional roux, and eventually earned the nickname as the "Cook of Kings, and the King of Cooks."

Cuisine Classique & Escoffier

Cuisine Classique was started by Auguste Escoffier, and is a simplified version of Grande Cusine. Cuisine Classique consited of less courses and focused more on culinary principles and teqniques, rather than presentation. Escoffier was the first to sort sauces into the five catergories, and earned the nickname as the "Emperor of the World's Kitchens."

Mid 20th-Early 21st Centuries

Nouvelle Cuisine

Nouvelle Cuisine was started in the 1960's by Ferdinand Point, and later was refined by his pupils. Nouvelle Cusine focused on simple cooking and emphasized healthiness, showing the importance of fresh and high-quality ingredients. This was also the beginning of home cooking out of enjoyment not necessity.

Ethinic Cusine

m

Ethinic Cuisine in America began with the Immigration and Nationality Act of 1965, which increased immigration from Asia and Latin America. It gained traction in the 1970's because Americans realized how much other cultures had to offer food wise.

California or New American Cusine

New American Cuisine emerged in the 1980's and is an Americanized Nouvlle Cuisine that combined traditional American teqniques with flavors from around the world. Much like Novelle Cuisine, New American also prioritized fresh ingredients, and coined the term farm-to-table, because the ingredients could be traced back to their origins easily. The most famous example of New American Cuisine is Alice Water's Chez Panisse in Berkeley, CA.

Modernist Cuisine

Modernist Cuisine began because both scientists and chefs wanted to know why different cooking processes effect ingredients differently on a molecular level. This led to Molecular Gastronomy. With an understanding of chemistry and physics in food, a chef can change the textures and shapes of food, making it an unexpected experience for the customer.
